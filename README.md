# Climatic Variability, Aridity and Drought Regime Changes in Southeastern Türkiye

## A NASA POWER-Based Hydroclimatological Assessment of Climate Variability, Aridity Intensification and Drought Regime Changes (1991–2025)

**Author: Ahmet Solmaz**

---

## 1. Study Overview

Climate change in Southeastern Türkiye is increasingly expressed not only through changes in temperature and precipitation, but also through **increasing atmospheric evaporative demand, aridity intensification, longer dry periods and changes in drought characteristics**.

This research investigates the changing hydroclimatic structure of Southeastern Türkiye during **1991–2025** using daily NASA POWER climate data.

The study focuses on nine provinces:

**Diyarbakır · Batman · Siirt · Kilis · Gaziantep · Şanlıurfa · Adıyaman · Şırnak · Mardin**

The main objective is to determine whether the region is undergoing a measurable transition toward a **warmer, more arid and increasingly drought-prone hydroclimatic regime**.

Rather than examining temperature or precipitation independently, this study evaluates their interaction with **potential evapotranspiration (PET), aridity, drought indices, dry spells and climatic regime shifts**.

---

# 2. Why Was This Study Conducted?

Southeastern Türkiye is one of the most climatically sensitive regions of Türkiye because of its:

* semi-arid to dry-subhumid climatic conditions,
* high summer temperatures,
* strong seasonal precipitation variability,
* dependence on water resources for agriculture,
* sensitivity to drought,
* and increasing atmospheric water demand.

A decrease in precipitation alone does not fully explain drought development.

Even when precipitation does not show a strong long-term decline, increasing temperature can increase **potential evapotranspiration**, resulting in greater atmospheric moisture demand and more severe water stress.

Therefore, this study was designed to answer several key questions:

1. **Is temperature significantly increasing across Southeastern Türkiye?**
2. **Are precipitation characteristics changing?**
3. **Is atmospheric evaporative demand increasing?**
4. **Is regional aridity intensifying?**
5. **Are dry spells becoming longer?**
6. **Are drought conditions becoming more strongly controlled by temperature and PET?**
7. **Did statistically significant climate regime shifts occur during 1991–2025?**
8. **Are the detected changes statistically significant as well as physically meaningful?**

# 4. Data

## NASA POWER Climate Data

Daily climate data were obtained from the **NASA POWER** database for the period:

**1991–2025**

Main climate variables used in the analysis include:

* **T2M** — Air temperature at 2 m
* **PRECTOTCORR** — Corrected precipitation
* **PET** — Potential evapotranspiration derived for hydroclimatic analysis

The daily data were processed and aggregated into appropriate monthly and annual time series.

The resulting datasets were subsequently used for trend analysis, change-point detection, drought analysis and aridity assessment.

# 5. Analytical Framework

The analysis combines several complementary statistical and hydroclimatological approaches.

### Trend Analysis

* Mann–Kendall trend test
* Sen's slope estimator
* Linear trend analysis

### Change-Point and Regime Analysis

* Pettitt change-point test
* PELT change-point detection
* OLS-CUSUM analysis

### Drought Analysis

* SPI-12
* SPEI-12
* Run-theory drought event analysis
* Drought frequency
* Drought duration
* Drought severity

### Aridity Analysis

* De Martonne Aridity Index
* UNEP Aridity Index

### Dry-Spell Analysis

* Consecutive Dry Days (CDD)
* Annual dry-spell characteristics
* Seasonal dry-spell characteristics

### Statistical Relationship Analysis

* Pearson correlation

* Climate–drought relationship analysis

### Effect Size

* Cohen's *d*
* Decadal Sen's slope rates

The combination of these methods allows the study to distinguish between **simple trends, abrupt regime shifts, drought responses and the magnitude of climatic changes**.

# 6. Main Findings

The results indicate a clear transformation of the hydroclimatic conditions across Southeastern Türkiye.

### 6.1 Warming

A persistent warming tendency was detected across the study region.

The temperature results demonstrate that warming is one of the most spatially consistent signals in the dataset.

This warming is particularly important because it increases atmospheric evaporative demand and can amplify drought conditions even where precipitation trends are comparatively weak.

---

### 6.2 Increasing Atmospheric Water Demand

The increase in temperature is accompanied by increasing **potential evapotranspiration (PET)**.

This indicates that the atmosphere is demanding more water from the land surface.

Consequently, hydroclimatic stress cannot be evaluated using precipitation alone.

---

### 6.3 Aridity Intensification

Both **De Martonne** and **UNEP P/PET** approaches indicate increasing atmospheric dryness in several provinces.

The results suggest that the regional hydroclimatic system is shifting toward conditions characterized by:

**higher temperature → higher PET → greater atmospheric water demand → increasing aridity pressure**

This represents an important climate-change signal for a region already exposed to water scarcity.

---

### 6.4 Increasing Dry-Spells

The Consecutive Dry Days (CDD) analysis indicates changes in the persistence of precipitation-free periods.

Increasing dry-spell duration is particularly important for:

* agricultural water demand,
* soil moisture availability,
* vegetation stress,
* irrigation requirements,
* and drought development.

---

### 6.5 SPI-12 and SPEI-12 Show Different Drought Signals

One of the important findings of the study is the difference between **SPI-12** and **SPEI-12**.

SPI primarily evaluates precipitation anomalies, whereas SPEI incorporates the effect of atmospheric water demand through the water-balance framework.

The results indicate that drought conditions identified by SPEI-12 can be stronger or more persistent than those identified by SPI-12.

This suggests that **temperature and increasing evaporative demand are becoming increasingly important components of regional drought development**.

---

### 6.6 Climatic Regime Shifts

Pettitt, PELT and OLS-CUSUM analyses identify periods where the statistical behaviour of climate variables changes.

These regime shifts indicate that the observed climate change is not necessarily represented by a single linear trend.

Instead, some variables show evidence of **structural changes in their temporal behaviour**.

---

### 6.7 Effect Size

Cohen's *d* analysis was used to evaluate whether detected changes were only statistically significant or also substantial in magnitude.

This provides an additional dimension to the interpretation of climate regime changes.

The combination of:

**trend significance + slope magnitude + regime shift + effect size**

provides a more robust assessment than relying on a single statistical test.

# 15. Scientific Interpretation

The combined results suggest that the hydroclimatic transformation of Southeastern Türkiye cannot be explained by precipitation variability alone.

The dominant pattern can be summarized as:

> **Warming → increasing PET → increasing atmospheric water demand → increasing aridity pressure → longer dry periods → increasing temperature-sensitive drought risk**

This chain is particularly important for a semi-arid region where small changes in water availability can produce substantial environmental and socioeconomic consequences.

The findings have implications for:

* agricultural drought,
* water-resource management,
* irrigation planning,
* climate adaptation,
* ecosystem vulnerability,
* land degradation,
* and regional environmental planning.
